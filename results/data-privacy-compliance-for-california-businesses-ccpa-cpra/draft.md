# Executive Brief: CCPA/CPRA Compliance Posture

**To:** Senior Leadership | **Re:** California privacy exposure and 2026–2028 priorities
**Note:** Based on research with a knowledge cutoff. Items marked *(verify)* need confirmation with counsel before action.

---

## Headline Takeaway

**California privacy enforcement is active, and expanding in scope.** Regulators are fining companies for broken opt-out tools and ignored browser signals. New rules on AI-driven decisions, risk assessments, and cybersecurity audits begin **January 1, 2026**. Compliance is now an operational discipline, not a one-time policy exercise.

---

## Key Findings

- **Broad coverage.** We are likely in scope if we do business in California and meet any one of these: revenue above ~$26.6M *(verify)*, data on 100,000+ consumers or households, or 50%+ of revenue from selling or sharing data.
- **Employee and B2B data are in scope.** The exemptions expired in 2023.
- **Two enforcers.** The Attorney General and the California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) both act. The mandatory 30-day cure period is gone.
- **Penalties scale.** Fines run ~$2,663 per violation and ~$7,988 for intentional violations or those involving minors *(verify)*. They are counted per consumer, so exposure grows with the number of people affected.
- **Settlements are rising.** Examples include Sephora ($1.2M), Honda (~$632K), and Healthline (~$1.55M, the largest at the time). Recurring causes:
  - Ignored Global Privacy Control signals
  - Confusing or asymmetric opt-out flows
  - Excessive identity verification
  - Weak vendor contracts
  - Children's data
- **New rules are coming** *(dates to verify)*:
  - **Jan 2026:** Risk assessment requirements begin.
  - **Jan 2027:** Automated decision-making (ADMT) notice, opt-out, and access rights.
  - **2028–2030:** Annual independent cybersecurity audits, phased by revenue.
  - **Aug 2026:** Data brokers must process deletions through the state's DROP platform.

---

## Key Risks

1. **Opt-out and tracker failures.** This is the most frequent enforcement theme, and a likely first target.
2. **Vendor and ad-tech contracts.** Missing terms can turn a "service provider" into a "sale" or "share."
3. **Data breach litigation.** Statutory damages of $100–$750 per consumer, per incident, scale quickly.
4. **Parallel wiretap claims.** Suits under the California Invasion of Privacy Act (CIPA) over website trackers are a separate exposure, with uncertain reform.
5. **AI and automated decisions.** Inventories, assessments, and opt-out mechanisms must be built before the deadlines.
6. **Minors' data.** Higher penalties and opt-in consent requirements apply.
7. **Regulatory flux.** Thresholds, rules, and enforcement priorities keep shifting.
