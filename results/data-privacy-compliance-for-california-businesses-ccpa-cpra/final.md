# Executive Brief: CCPA/CPRA Compliance Posture

**To:** Senior Leadership | **Re:** California privacy exposure and 2026–2030 priorities
**Note:** This brief rests on research with a knowledge cutoff. Items marked *(verify)* need confirmation with counsel before action. Settlement amounts, 2025 adjusted figures, and phase-in dates are recalled figures, not confirmed against primary sources.

---

## Headline Takeaway

**California privacy enforcement is active, and new obligations are phasing in.** Regulators have fined companies for broken opt-out tools and ignored browser signals. New regulations on risk assessments, cybersecurity audits, and automated decision-making technology (ADMT) take effect **January 1, 2026**, with most obligations phasing in from 2026 through 2030 *(verify)*. Compliance is now an operational discipline, not a one-time policy exercise.

---

## Key Findings

- **Broad coverage.** We are in scope if we do business in California, are a for-profit entity, and meet any one of these:
  - Revenue above ~$26.6M *(verify; originally $25M, adjusted for inflation)*.
  - Buy, sell, or share data on 100,000+ California consumers or households.
  - 50%+ of revenue from selling or sharing personal information.
- **Employee and B2B data are in scope.** The exemptions expired on Jan 1, 2023, and extensions failed.
- **Two enforcers.** The Attorney General and the California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) both act. The mandatory 30-day cure period for administrative enforcement is gone. Cure is now at the regulator's discretion.
- **Penalties scale.** Statutory penalties are $2,500 per violation and $7,500 per intentional violation or violation involving minors under 16, adjusted for CPI. The 2025 adjusted figures are about $2,663 and $7,988 *(verify)*. Penalties are counted per violation, so exposure can grow with the number of consumers affected.
- **Enforcement actions to date.** Examples include Sephora ($1.2M, AG, 2022), Honda (~$632K, CPPA, 2025 *(verify)*), and Healthline (~$1.55M, AG, 2025 *(verify)*). Healthline was reported as the largest CCPA settlement at that time. Recurring causes:
  - Ignored Global Privacy Control (GPC) signals
  - Confusing or asymmetric opt-out flows
  - Excessive identity verification
  - Weak vendor contracts
  - Children's data
- **New rules are phasing in** *(dates to verify)*:
  - **Jan 1, 2026:** Regulations effective. Risk assessment requirements begin.
  - **Aug 2026:** Registered data brokers must begin processing deletion requests through the state's DROP platform.
  - **Jan 2027:** ADMT notice, opt-out, and access rights for "significant decisions" (e.g., lending, housing, education, employment, healthcare).
  - **2028–2030:** Annual independent cybersecurity audits, phased by revenue. The first deadline is about April 2028 for the largest companies.
  - **~April 2028:** Risk assessment attestations or summaries due to the CPPA.

---

## Key Risks

1. **Opt-out and tracker failures (GPC, cookie configuration).** This is the most frequent enforcement theme.
2. **Vendor and ad-tech contracts.** Missing required terms can turn a "service provider" relationship into a "sale" or "share."
3. **Employee/HR and B2B data.** Often overlooked since the exemptions expired.
4. **Data breach litigation.** The private right of action covers certain data breaches. Statutory damages of $100–$750 per consumer, per incident, scale quickly.
5. **Parallel wiretap claims.** Suits under the California Invasion of Privacy Act (CIPA) over website trackers are a separate, non-CCPA exposure. The status of reform efforts is uncertain.
6. **AI and automated decisions.** Inventories, risk assessments, and opt-out and access mechanisms must be built ahead of the 2026–2027 phase-in dates.
7. **Minors' data.** Higher penalties and opt-in consent requirements apply. The status of the California Age-Appropriate Design Code is uncertain because of ongoing litigation.
8. **Regulatory flux.** Thresholds adjust for CPI, regulations are evolving, and enforcement priorities shift.

## Recommended Actions

Timing below is management's proposed sequencing. It is not a legal deadline unless a regulatory date is stated.

| Priority | Action | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Audit websites and apps** for GPC handling, cookie banners, and "Do Not Sell or Share" and "Limit Use" links. Fix asymmetric choice designs. | Next 30–60 days |
| 2 | **Review vendor, ad-tech, and third-party contracts** for required CCPA terms. | 60–90 days |
| 3 | **Confirm applicability and data mapping,** including HR, applicant, contractor, and B2B data, and any exemption claims. | 60–90 days |
| 4 | **Stress-test request handling:** 10-business-day acknowledgment, 45-day response (extendable by 45), 15-business-day opt-out/limit processing, and minimal verification *(verify the business-day timelines)*. | Within the next quarter |
| 5 | **Build an AI/ADMT inventory** and start a risk assessment program. | Risk assessments: the Jan 1, 2026 effective date (start immediately if passed). ADMT: by Jan 2027 |
| 6 | **Plan for cybersecurity audits.** Review breach readiness and "reasonable security" controls. | Budget now; audit deadline 2028–2030 depending on revenue |
| 7 | **Assign executive ownership** and monitor CPPA/AG actions and pending legislation (e.g., AB 566 browser opt-out signal, operative ~Jan 2027 *(verify)*). | Ongoing |
