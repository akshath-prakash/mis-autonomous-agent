# Executive Brief: Supply Chain Visibility (SCV) in Retail

**Prepared for:** Senior Leadership | **Basis:** Desk research from background knowledge, not live retrieval. Items marked *verify* need confirmation against primary sources before we commit funds.

---

## Headline Takeaway

**Visibility technology is mature where we can already see (transportation tracking, apparel RFID) and weak where risk concentrates (sub-tier suppliers, data quality, partner interoperability). Regulation and retailer mandates are driving adoption, but their dates keep shifting. We should invest in durable foundations (data standards, supplier integration, decision rules) rather than build to any single compliance deadline.**

---

## Key Findings

- **Proven capabilities:** Multimodal shipment tracking is mainstream among large retailers. Item-level RFID is established in apparel and footwear (Walmart, Target, Inditex). Inventory accuracy gains of roughly 60–75% to 95%+ are widely cited, but figures vary by study (*indicative only*).
- **Persistent gaps:** Tier-2+ supplier opacity, inconsistent master data, siloed ERP/WMS/TMS systems, uneven GS1/EPCIS adoption, and unreliable ocean carrier data.
- **Demand drivers:** UFLPA enforcement, FSMA 204 food traceability, EU product passport and due-diligence rules, tariff and de minimis changes, and repeated disruptions (Red Sea, Panama Canal).
- **Key milestones:**
  - **~2027:** GS1 Sunrise 2027, with point-of-sale 2D barcode readiness.
  - **~2028:** FSMA 204 (extension announced; *verify legal status*).
  - **2026–2028+:** Phased EU Digital Product Passport requirements, with textile timing uncertain.
- **Emerging technology:** AI and agentic control towers are in early production. Vendor claims run ahead of reality.

---

## Principal Risks

1. **Regulatory instability:** EU and US rules (FSMA 204, EUDR, CSDDD) are being delayed or revised, so we risk building to moving targets.
2. **Execution at scale:** Pilots succeed, but supplier compliance, tag placement errors, and store process drift undermine rollout.
3. **Cybersecurity:** Greater connectivity widens the attack surface. M&S, Co-op, and UNFI incidents in 2025 show the exposure.
4. **Technology and vendor bets:** TradeLens was discontinued, and vendor consolidation (e.g., WiseTech/E2open, *verify*) can change roadmaps and pricing.
5. **Privacy and AI reliance:** Item-level tracking raises GDPR concerns. Unvalidated AI predictions can mislead decisions.
6. **Unproven ROI:** Reliable market-size, cost-per-tag, and ROI figures are not available. Vendor claims are rarely independently verified.

---

## Recommended Actions

1. **Fix the data foundation first.** Standardize product and location master data on GS1 identifiers (GTIN, GLN, SSCC) and set EPCIS-ready data requirements for new supplier contracts.
2. **Prioritize by value and obligation.** Scale RFID where economics are proven (apparel, high-value goods). Run controlled pilots in other categories with predefined success metrics.
3. **Prepare for Sunrise 2027.** Assess POS and e-commerce readiness for 2D barcodes now. Confirm peer retailers' commitments.
4. **Build modular, regulation-agnostic traceability.** Map tier-1 to tier-n for high-risk categories (UFLPA exposure, food, textiles) without locking into a single compliance date. Maintain a regulatory watch with a named owner.
5. **Reduce vendor and technology risk.** Favor open standards and API-based architectures, include exit and data-portability terms in contracts, and avoid unproven technologies such as blockchain.
6. **Embed security and privacy by design.** Require third-party security assessments and run a privacy review of consumer-linked tracking.
7. **Govern AI use.** Pilot AI exception management with human oversight, defined decision rules, and alert-volume targets.
8. **Close the evidence gaps within 60–90 days.** Validate current legal dates, retailer mandate scopes, tag and reader costs, and ROI benchmarks through primary sources (GS1, FDA, EU Official Journal, CBP, Auburn RFID Lab, supplier portals) before final budget approval.

---

**Decision requested:** Approve foundational data and standards work and a 60–90 day verification sprint, with scaled investment gated on validated costs, dates, and pilot results.
