# Executive Brief: Northern California Data Center Power Constraints

**Basis:** This brief draws on analyst knowledge without live source access. The direction of the findings is reliable. Specific figures, dates, and regulatory statuses are unverified and must be confirmed before external use.

## Headline Takeaway
**Power availability, not land or capital, now gates new data center capacity in Northern California.** Expect multi-year energization waits in the Bay Area core. Plan for delayed timelines, geographic diversification, and bridging power strategies.

## Key Findings
- **The market spans several utilities.** PG&E serves San Jose, the East Bay, the Peninsula, and the Central Valley. Silicon Valley Power (Santa Clara) and SMUD (Sacramento) are separate, with their own constraints and processes.
- **Demand is outpacing the grid.** Large-load requests are growing faster than transmission and substation capacity. PG&E has cited a multi-gigawatt pipeline (I recall roughly 10 GW, unverified). These are requests, not commitments, and the share likely to be built is unknown.
- **Timelines are long.** Developers report multi-year waits, with some energization possibly in the late 2020s to early 2030s (unverified). Major transmission upgrades often take 5+ years, and transformer shortages and permitting add delay.
- **Rules are tightening.** PG&E and the CPUC are pursuing large-load interconnection reform, including cluster studies and financial commitments, to filter out speculative requests. The current status is unconfirmed.
- **Supply and cost pressures compound.** Diablo Canyon is extended to about 2029–30. The January 2025 Moss Landing fire removed a large block of storage. California's high retail rates make cost allocation to new large loads politically sensitive.
- **Developers are adapting.** They are exploring on-site generation, storage, flexible load, and sites in the Central Valley and Sacramento regions.

## Key Risks
- **Schedule:** Energization delays could strand land, equipment, and customer commitments.
- **Cost:** Regulators are focused on shielding existing ratepayers, so expect higher deposits, collateral, and minimum-take requirements.
- **Reliability:** Peak-demand stress, wildfire-related outages and shutoffs, and storage or generation outages.
- **Permitting:** Bay Area air district limits on backup diesel generation and local siting opposition.
- **Policy:** 2025 state bills on data center energy use (SB 57, SB 58, AB 222) have unknown outcomes.
- **Demand and supply chain:** AI load forecasts vary widely, and transformer and switchgear lead times remain long.

## Recommended Actions
1. **Verify the facts within two weeks.** Confirm PG&E's current pipeline, the CPUC large-load proceeding, CAISO-approved projects, and the status of the 2025 bills. Primary sources are earnings transcripts, CPUC dockets, CAISO plans, and legislative records.
2. **Engage utilities now.** Request written, site-specific energization timelines from PG&E, SVP, or SMUD. Do not rely on market anecdotes.
3. **Diversify the site portfolio.** Evaluate Central Valley and Sacramento options alongside the Bay Area core.
4. **Develop a bridging power strategy.** Assess on-site generation, storage, and curtailable-load agreements, and their air-permit implications.
5. **Prepare for financial commitments.** Budget for deposits and collateral, and prioritize projects with firm customer demand.
6. **Set monitoring triggers.** Track CPUC rulings, legislative action, and PG&E quarterly updates, and revisit the plan when any of them changes.
