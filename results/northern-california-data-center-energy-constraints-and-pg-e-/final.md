# Executive Brief: Northern California Data Center Power Constraints

**Basis and reliability:** This brief draws on analyst knowledge with no live source access, so it may be out of date. Items are labeled **[HIGH]**, **[MED]**, or **[UNVERIFIED]** according to the research confidence rating. Figures, dates, and regulatory statuses are unverified and must be confirmed against primary sources before any external use.

## Headline Takeaway
**Power availability is a primary gating constraint on new data center capacity in Northern California, alongside land and local permitting limits.** Some projects in PG&E territory, especially around San Jose, have reportedly been told to expect multi-year energization waits [MED]. Plan for delayed timelines, geographic diversification, and bridging power strategies.

## Key Findings
- **The market spans several utilities [HIGH].**
  - PG&E serves much of the Bay Area, including most of San Jose, the East Bay, and the Peninsula. It also serves Central Valley and Sacramento-adjacent areas such as Tracy and Stockton.
  - Silicon Valley Power (the City of Santa Clara's municipal utility) is separate from PG&E and faces its own capacity and import constraints.
  - SMUD (Sacramento) is a separate and growing market. Its specific capacity limits are unconfirmed.
- **Demand is outpacing the grid in parts of the Bay Area [HIGH].** Large-load requests have grown faster than transmission and substation capacity, and developers report long waits for new service.
  - PG&E has publicly described a multi-gigawatt pipeline of data center requests [MED]. The exact current figure is **[UNVERIFIED]**.
  - These are requests, not firm commitments. The share likely to be built and energized is unknown.
- **Timelines are long [MED].** Multi-year waits have been reported for some projects. Specific energization dates are **[UNVERIFIED]** and are not cited here.
  - Major transmission and substation upgrades commonly take several years, often 5+ for major transmission [MED, general industry knowledge].
  - Transformer supply constraints, permitting, and CEQA review add delay [MED].
- **Interconnection rules may be tightening [MED].** PG&E has proposed or pursued a more structured large-load process, including cluster-style studies and financial commitments, to filter speculative requests. This is associated with a CPUC proceeding and "Electric Rule 30." The rule number, docket, and status (proposed vs. approved) are **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Supply and cost pressures compound.**
  - Diablo Canyon's operation has been extended by state law (SB 846) to roughly 2029–30 [HIGH]. Federal licensing status is unconfirmed.
  - The January 2025 Moss Landing (Vistra) battery fire removed a large block of storage in the PG&E area [HIGH]. The current capacity impact and recovery timeline are unknown.
  - California IOU retail rates are among the highest in the US, which makes cost allocation for new large loads politically sensitive [HIGH]. There is an active policy concern that existing ratepayers could bear data-center-driven upgrade costs [MED].
  - PG&E leadership has argued that load growth can put downward pressure on rates, and regulators and consumer advocates dispute this [MED]. The claimed magnitude is **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Developers are adapting [MED].** They are exploring on-site generation (e.g., fuel cells, gas turbines), battery storage, and flexible or curtailable load to speed interconnection. They are also looking at the East Bay, Central Valley, and Sacramento region [MED]. Specific projects, capacities, and approvals are **[UNVERIFIED]**.

## Key Risks
- **Schedule (analyst inference):** Energization delays could strand land, equipment, and customer commitments.
- **Cost [MED]:** Existing-ratepayer protection is a live regulatory concern, and stranded costs are possible if projects cancel after upgrades are funded. Regulators are addressing this through financial-commitment requirements, so developers may face higher deposits or collateral. Exact terms are unconfirmed.
- **Reliability [MED to HIGH]:** Summer evening peak and heat stress, wildfire-related outages and Public Safety Power Shutoffs in PG&E territory, and storage or generation outages.
- **PG&E financial health [HIGH on history; UNVERIFIED on current status]:** Wildfire liability affects PG&E's capital costs and its ability to fund upgrades. PG&E emerged from bankruptcy in 2020.
- **Permitting and siting [MED]:** Bay Area Air Quality Management District permitting for backup diesel generators, local land-use and permitting constraints in Santa Clara and San Jose, local opposition, and water use.
- **Policy [UNVERIFIED]:** Possible state restrictions, tariffs, or reporting mandates. 2025 bills on data center energy use (recalled as SB 57, SB 58, and AB 222) have unknown outcomes. Even the bill numbers and content are unverified recollection.
- **Demand and supply chain [MED]:** AI-driven load forecasts vary widely, and a slowdown in AI investment would sharply change the need. Transformer, switchgear, and breaker lead times are long.

## Recommended Actions
*These are management recommendations, not research findings.*
1. **Verify the facts promptly (suggested target: two weeks).** Confirm the following:
   - PG&E's current pipeline figure and firm share
   - the CPUC large-load proceeding and its status
   - CAISO-approved projects tied to Bay Area load
   - Diablo Canyon licensing and Moss Landing status
   - the outcomes of the 2025 bills

   Primary sources are PG&E earnings transcripts, CPUC dockets, CAISO Transmission Plans, CEC IEPR forecasts, and the California Legislative Information site.
2. **Engage utilities now.** Request written, site-specific energization timelines from PG&E, SVP, or SMUD. Do not rely on market anecdotes.
3. **Diversify the site portfolio.** Evaluate East Bay, Central Valley, and Sacramento options alongside the Bay Area core, and confirm each utility's current capacity limits.
4. **Develop a bridging power strategy.** Assess on-site generation, storage, and curtailable-load arrangements, along with their air-permit implications.
5. **Prepare for financial commitments.** Budget for possible deposits and collateral, and prioritize projects with firm customer demand.
6. **Set monitoring triggers.** Track CPUC rulings, legislative action, and PG&E quarterly updates, and revisit the plan when any of them changes.
