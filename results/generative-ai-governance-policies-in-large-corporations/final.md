# Executive Brief: Generative AI Governance

**Headline:** Early precedent holds companies responsible for what their AI says and does. The rules are fragmented and still shifting. We recommend moving beyond policy documents to enforced, risk-tiered governance, built to the strictest applicable standard.

*Evidence note: This brief draws on research from training knowledge, with no live verification. Items marked "verify" must be confirmed before external use. Anything dated late 2025 or later is especially unconfirmed.*

---

## Key Findings

- **The EU AI Act is a major driver of corporate policy.**
  - Prohibited-practice and AI-literacy duties have applied since Feb 2025. General-purpose AI obligations have applied since Aug 2025 (exact dates: verify).
  - High-risk and transparency duties are scheduled for Aug 2026, and for Aug 2027 for AI embedded in regulated products.
  - Fines for prohibited practices reach €35M or 7% of global turnover. Lower tiers apply to other breaches.
  - A proposed "Digital Omnibus" (late 2025) reportedly seeks to delay or soften some high-risk deadlines. Its status is unconfirmed (verify).
- **The US is a patchwork.**
  - There is no comprehensive federal AI statute.
  - State laws include the Colorado AI Act (effective 30 June 2026, and further change is possible). California (SB 53, AB 2013) and Texas (TRAIGA) are effective around Jan 2026. All three dates need verification.
  - NYC Local Law 144 (automated hiring tools) has been enforced since 2023.
  - A reported late-2025 federal effort to challenge or preempt state laws is unverified, and its durability is uncertain.
- **Accountability has precedent.**
  - In *Moffatt* (2024), Air Canada was held responsible for its chatbot's statements.
  - The SEC has brought "AI-washing" enforcement actions, such as the March 2024 cases against Delphia and Global Predictions. These bear on corporate AI disclosures.
- **Peer practice has matured.** Many firms moved from 2023 restrictions to governed deployment. Common elements are approved enterprise tools, data-classification rules, human review, cross-functional oversight, use-case inventories and vendor diligence.
- **Voluntary standards are common reference points.** NIST AI RMF (with the Generative AI Profile, AI 600-1) and ISO/IEC 42001 (certifiable) are widely referenced.
- **Shadow AI is a key practical constraint.** One 2024 survey (Microsoft/LinkedIn Work Trend Index) reportedly found roughly 75–80% of AI users bring their own tools to work. **This figure is unverified (LOW confidence); do not use externally until confirmed.**

## Key Risks

- **Regulatory:** Shifting dates risk over- or under-investment, and EU penalties are large.
- **Data and IP:** Leakage of trade secrets or personal data, and unresolved copyright litigation.
- **Liability:** Hallucinations in customer-facing or decision-making use.
- **Bias:** Exposure in hiring, lending and insurance.
- **Security:** Prompt injection and agentic systems exceeding their permissions.
- **Reputation and disclosure:** Overstated AI claims and public incidents.
- **Governance:** "Paper compliance" without enforcement, shadow AI, and dependence on a few model vendors.

## Recommended Actions

*These are management recommendations drawn from the research. They are not research findings. Timelines are proposals.*

1. **Confirm the regulatory timeline (proposed: 30 days).** Have counsel verify the Digital Omnibus status and the Colorado, California and Texas dates.
2. **Inventory and tier all AI use,** including shadow AI, by risk level.
3. **Align to NIST AI RMF.** Evaluate ISO 42001 certification. Apply the strictest applicable standard globally. The EU AI Act is a likely candidate, but counsel should confirm.
4. **Enable safe alternatives.** Provide enterprise-licensed tools with no-training and data-retention terms. Back them with technical controls such as AI gateways and data-loss prevention.
5. **Assign ownership.** Name an accountable executive and a cross-functional AI committee, with regular board reporting.
6. **Strengthen vendor contracts** with IP indemnities, model-change notification and audit rights.
7. **Require human review** for consequential decisions, and legal review of external AI claims.
8. **Set guardrails for agentic AI** before broad rollout. These cover permissions, audit trails and shutdown capability. No settled standard exists yet, so treat this as emerging practice.

## Open Gaps

- We lack verified benchmarks on peer adoption (policies, governance committees, Chief AI Officers), enforcement outcomes and program cost.
- Before any board presentation:
  - Pull current Gartner, McKinsey, IBM and Deloitte survey data, with dates.
  - Confirm the status of major copyright cases (e.g., NYT v. OpenAI).
  - Confirm the Digital Omnibus outcome, US federal preemption status and any 2026 legislative changes.
