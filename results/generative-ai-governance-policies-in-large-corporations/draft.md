# Executive Brief: Generative AI Governance

**Headline:** Companies are accountable for what their AI says and does, and the rules are fragmented and still shifting. We should replace policy documents with enforced, risk-tiered governance now, built to the strictest applicable standard.

*Evidence note: This brief draws on research from training knowledge, with no live verification. Items marked "verify" must be confirmed before external use.*

---

## Key Findings

- **EU AI Act is the global benchmark.** Prohibited-practice and AI-literacy duties have applied since Feb 2025, and general-purpose AI obligations since Aug 2025. High-risk and transparency duties are scheduled for Aug 2026. Fines reach €35M or 7% of global turnover. A proposed "Digital Omnibus" may delay some deadlines (status unconfirmed, verify).
- **The US is a patchwork.** There is no federal AI statute. Colorado (scheduled June 2026), California and Texas (~Jan 2026) are advancing state laws (verify dates). Federal preemption efforts are uncertain.
- **Accountability is established.** A Canadian tribunal held Air Canada liable for its chatbot's statements, and the SEC has pursued "AI-washing" in corporate disclosures.
- **Peer practice has matured.** Many firms moved from 2023 bans to governed deployment, with approved enterprise tools, data-classification rules, human review, cross-functional oversight, use-case inventories and vendor diligence.
- **Voluntary standards are the common reference points.** NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001 (certifiable) are widely used.
- **Shadow AI is the biggest practical gap.** One 2024 survey reportedly found roughly 75–80% of AI users bring their own tools to work (unverified).

## Key Risks

- **Regulatory:** Shifting dates risk over- or under-investment, and EU penalties are severe.
- **Data and IP:** Leakage of trade secrets or personal data, and unresolved copyright litigation.
- **Liability:** Hallucinations in customer-facing or decision-making use.
- **Bias:** Exposure in hiring, lending and insurance.
- **Security:** Prompt injection and agentic systems exceeding their permissions.
- **Reputation:** Overstated AI claims and public incidents.
- **Governance:** "Paper compliance" without enforcement, and dependence on a few model vendors.

## Recommended Actions

1. **Confirm the timeline (30 days).** Have counsel verify the EU Omnibus status and the Colorado, California and Texas dates.
2. **Inventory and tier all AI use,** including shadow AI, by risk level.
3. **Standardize on NIST AI RMF.** Evaluate ISO 42001 certification, and apply the strictest standard (EU) globally.
4. **Enable safe alternatives.** Provide enterprise-licensed tools with no-training and retention terms, backed by technical controls such as AI gateways and data-loss prevention.
5. **Assign ownership.** Name an accountable executive and a cross-functional AI committee with regular board reporting.
6. **Strengthen vendor contracts** with IP indemnities, model-change notification and audit rights.
7. **Require human review** for consequential decisions, and legal review of external AI claims.
8. **Set guardrails for agentic AI,** covering permissions, audit trails and shutdown capability, before broad rollout.
