# Executive Brief: ERP Migration Risk for Mid-Sized Manufacturers

**Evidence note:** This brief draws on general industry knowledge, not verified current data. Vendor dates, statistics, and case figures must be confirmed against primary sources before any decision or external citation.

## Headline Takeaway

**Vendor pressure is making ERP migration a question of when, not whether. The main danger is execution, not the decision itself.** Data quality, over-customization, and cutover disruption are the most commonly cited causes of failure. Mid-sized firms have less financial buffer to absorb them.

## Key Findings

- **Vendors are pushing customers to the cloud.** SAP, Microsoft, Oracle, and Infor are directing new investment, especially AI features, to cloud editions. *(High confidence on direction.)*
- **Support deadlines are approaching.** SAP ECC 6.0 mainstream maintenance is reported to end in 2027, and Dynamics GP around 2029. *(Medium to low confidence. Verify with vendors.)*
- **Timelines are long.** Mid-market projects are commonly cited at 9–18 months, plus selection time. Firms facing 2027–2030 deadlines need to start planning early. *(Uncertain range; start dates are firm-specific.)*
- **Mid-sized firms are structurally exposed.** They typically have small IT teams, heavy reliance on integrators, little capacity to backfill staff, and a shortage of skilled consultants. *(Mostly high confidence.)*
- **Regulated sectors face longer projects.** FDA, ISO, AS9100, and CMMC requirements add validation and documentation work. *(High confidence.)*
- **Failure data is unreliable.** Surveys report frequent overruns and missed benefits, but figures vary widely and come largely from consultancies. No reliable percentage is available to cite.

## Top Risks

1. **Scope creep and over-customization:** raises cost and complicates future upgrades.
2. **Poor data quality:** BOMs, routings, item masters, and inventory balances are frequent causes of go-live problems.
3. **Cutover disruption:** MRP, scheduling, or inventory errors can cause missed shipments or stockouts. Hershey, Nike, and Revlon are cited as examples. *(Details unverified.)*
4. **Fragile integrations:** MES, PLM, WMS, EDI, and quality systems.
5. **Weak change management and sponsorship:** inadequate training and thin executive backing.
6. **Partner dependence and pricing shifts:** subscription models may raise long-term total cost of ownership.

## Recommended Actions

1. **Confirm the clock.** Verify current vendor end-of-support dates and regulatory deadlines (e.g., e-invoicing) for our regions.
2. **Start data cleanup now.** Assign data owners and plan multiple mock migrations.
3. **Adopt "fit to standard."** Require executive approval for any customization.
4. **Secure sponsorship and capacity.** Name an executive sponsor and dedicate operations experts, with backfill.
5. **Reduce cutover risk.** Phase the rollout where feasible, build inventory buffers, rehearse cutover, and define a rollback plan. Avoid peak production and fiscal year-end.
6. **Structure partner contracts carefully.** Use milestone-based terms and clear support obligations, and consider independent project assurance.
7. **Define benefit metrics before go-live** and track them afterward.
