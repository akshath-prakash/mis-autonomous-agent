# Executive Brief: Cyber Insurance Market for SMBs

*Basis: analyst synthesis from knowledge through late 2025, with no live data. Figures are approximate and should be validated before external use.*

## Headline Takeaway
**Cyber coverage is cheaper and easier to get than at the 2021-22 peak, but access and claim payouts depend on proven security controls. The soft market is fragile, so we should secure favorable terms now and prepare for a possible reversal.**

## Key Findings
- **Pricing cycle:** After the hard market of 2020-22 (rate increases above 100% in some 2021 quarters), rates stabilized in 2023 and softened in 2024-25, typically by single to low double digits. *SMB-specific rate data is unverified.*
- **Market size:** Global premiums were about $15B in 2024 (Munich Re estimate). Forecasts of doubling by around 2030 are projections, not facts.
- **Controls are the gatekeeper:** Insurers expect MFA, EDR, tested offline backups, patching, email filtering, and an incident response plan. Missing MFA can mean a decline, a premium surcharge, or a ransomware sublimit. Outside-in scanning increasingly shapes quotes.
- **Coverage gaps persist:** Common limits include ransomware and funds-transfer-fraud sublimits, coinsurance, war and state-backed attack exclusions (Lloyd's, since March 2023), systemic-event limits, and "failure to maintain" conditions.
- **Distribution is shifting:** MGAs and insurtechs (Coalition, At-Bay, Cowbell) and MSP or software bundles dominate SMB sales. Travelers acquired Corvus in 2024.
- **Take-up is low:** Many SMBs wrongly assume they are not targets or that general liability covers cyber losses.

## Key Risks
- **Ransomware remains the top loss driver.** Reported payments fell in 2024 (about $814M vs. $1.25B), but that does not prove fewer attacks or lower business-interruption costs.
- **Supply-chain and systemic events** (MOVEit, Change Healthcare, the CrowdStrike outage) can hit SMBs through vendors, and dependent-business coverage is often thin.
- **Soft-market correction (our judgment, not a forecast):** A major event could bring higher rates, narrower terms, or reduced appetite.
- **Claim disputes:** Inaccurate security questionnaires risk policy rescission, and exclusions such as war remain legally contested.
- **Regulatory flux:** The CIRCIA final rule (delayed toward 2026), the HIPAA Security Rule update, EU NIS2/DORA/CRA obligations, and UK proposals all have unconfirmed timing.
- **AI-enabled threats** (deepfake fraud, sophisticated phishing) are not yet quantified in loss data.

## Recommended Actions
1. **Validate before committing.** Pull current Marsh, Aon, NAIC, Verizon DBIR, and MGA claims data to confirm SMB rate trends, take-up, and 2025 incident impacts.
2. **Close control gaps now.** Prioritize MFA, EDR, and tested offline backups. These drive insurability and price.
3. **Lock in favorable terms.** Open renewals early, and evaluate higher limits and longer commitments while pricing is soft.
4. **Audit policy language.** Review sublimits, war and systemic exclusions, dependent-business coverage, and "failure to maintain" clauses. Ensure applications are accurate.
5. **Map vendor dependencies** and assess exposure to critical cloud and software providers.
6. **Assign a regulatory owner** to track CIRCIA, HIPAA, and EU/UK developments.

## Known Gaps
Current SMB rates, loss ratios, market shares, AI-exclusion prevalence, and final regulatory status are unconfirmed. Treat them as items to verify, not conclusions.
