# Executive Brief: Cyber Insurance Market for SMBs

*Basis: This brief was prepared from training knowledge that thins out in late 2025. No live data was accessed, and nothing was checked against current sources. Figures are approximate and must be validated before external use. Confidence levels are noted where the research rated a claim below High.*

## Headline Takeaway
**Overall-market cyber pricing has eased from the 2021–22 peak. Access and claim outcomes still depend on demonstrable security controls. Softening may not last, so we recommend validating current SMB terms and closing control gaps now. We also recommend preparing for a possible reversal. This is our judgment, not a forecast.**

## Key Findings
- **Pricing cycle [Medium-High]:** The 2020–22 hard market saw sharp rate increases, with Marsh's global index showing increases above 100% in some 2021 quarters (exact figures uncertain). Rates stabilized in 2023 and softened in 2024–25. Reported decreases were typically single digits to low double digits. *SMB-specific rate data is unverified. SMB pricing generally follows the overall market but relies more on automated, scan-based underwriting.*
- **Market size [Medium]:** Global cyber premiums were roughly $15B in 2024 (Munich Re estimate, exact figure uncertain). Some industry forecasts project a doubling by around 2030. This is a projection, not a fact.
- **Controls act as the gatekeeper [High]:** Insurers commonly require or heavily weight the following:
  - MFA
  - EDR
  - Tested offline or immutable backups
  - Patch management
  - Email filtering
  - Incident response planning and training

  Without MFA, applicants may be declined, face large premium loads, or receive ransomware sublimits. Many carriers and MGAs use outside-in scanning in quoting, and findings can change price or eligibility.
- **Coverage gaps persist [High unless noted]:** Common limitations include:
  - Ransomware and funds-transfer-fraud sublimits
  - Coinsurance
  - War and state-backed attack exclusions (Lloyd's required them in new and renewing policies from 31 March 2023; wording varies by insurer)
  - Systemic-event and infrastructure exclusions or sublimits
  - "Failure to maintain" security conditions
- **Distribution [Medium-High]:** MGAs and insurtechs (e.g., Coalition, At-Bay, Cowbell) are prominent in SMB distribution, alongside large carriers such as Chubb, Beazley, and Travelers. Bundling with MSPs and software or security vendors is a growing trend. Travelers acquired Corvus in 2024. *Current market shares are uncertain.*
- **Take-up [Medium]:** SMB take-up remains well below that of large enterprises. Many SMBs wrongly believe they are not targets, or assume general liability or property policies cover cyber losses. Survey percentages vary widely and are uncertain.

## Key Risks
- **Ransomware remains the dominant loss driver [High].** Chainalysis estimated reported ransom payments fell in 2024 (about $814M vs. about $1.25B in 2023 [Medium]). Lower reported payments do not demonstrate fewer attacks or lower business-interruption costs.
- **Third-party and systemic events [Medium-High].**
  - Incidents such as MOVEit (2023) and Change Healthcare (2024) show SMBs can be hit through vendors.
  - The July 2024 CrowdStrike outage was a non-malicious event with large but widely varying insured-loss estimates (hundreds of millions to low billions USD, uncertain).
  - SMB policies may have limited dependent-business coverage.
- **Soft-market correction [Medium; our analytical judgment, not a prediction].** Falling rates while threats persist could lead to under-pricing. A major event could bring higher rates, narrower coverage, or reduced appetite for SMB buyers. Likelihood and timing are unknown.
- **Claim disputes [Medium].**
  - Inaccurate security questionnaires create rescission risk.
  - War-exclusion application is legally uncertain. In *Merck v. ACE* (NotPetya), a New Jersey court ruled against applying the exclusion to a cyberattack. Details and appeal status must be verified. *Mondelez v. Zurich* settled in 2022.
- **Regulatory developments [Medium; verify status of each].**
  - NYDFS Part 500 amendments were phased in through 1 Nov 2025 [High]. They affect NY-regulated financial entities and indirectly their vendors and SMB suppliers.
  - The CIRCIA final rule was expected in 2025, but our recollection is that it has been delayed toward 2026. The final timing and scope are uncertain.
  - The HIPAA Security Rule update was proposed in Jan 2025, and its finalization status is unknown.
  - In the EU, DORA has applied since 17 Jan 2025. NIS2 implementation is uneven across member states. Cyber Resilience Act reporting obligations are expected from about Sept 2026, and dates should be verified.
  - UK ransomware and cyber legislation outcomes and timing are uncertain.
- **AI-enabled threats [Medium].** Deepfake-driven fraud, more convincing phishing, and faster vulnerability exploitation are concerns. Their impact on loss frequency is not yet quantified.

## Recommended Actions
*Actions 2–6 reflect management judgment informed by the research. They are not findings drawn from verified data.*
1. **Validate before committing.** Pull current Marsh, Aon, WTW, NAIC, Verizon DBIR, and MGA claims data to confirm SMB rate trends, take-up, and 2025 incident impacts.
2. **Close control gaps now.** Prioritize MFA, EDR, and tested offline backups. These influence insurability and price.
3. **Engage on renewal terms early.** After step 1, evaluate whether current pricing and higher limits are favorable. Whether longer commitments are advisable is not supported by the research and needs broker input.
4. **Audit policy language.** Review sublimits, war and systemic exclusions, dependent-business coverage, and "failure to maintain" clauses. Ensure application answers are accurate.
5. **Map vendor dependencies.** Assess exposure to critical cloud and software providers.
6. **Assign a regulatory owner** to track CIRCIA, HIPAA, and EU/UK developments.

## Known Gaps
The following are unconfirmed and should be treated as items to verify, not conclusions:
- Current (2025–26) SMB rates, loss ratios, and take-up
- Market shares and exact premium and forecast figures
- Whether the soft market continues or reverses
- Prevalence of AI-related exclusions or endorsements
- Impact of major 2025 incidents on carrier appetite
- Final status and dates for CIRCIA, the HIPAA update, and UK legislation
